I put together a report comparing the current AI tools for making technical YouTube videos as of October 2026. It looks at avatar clones versus filming yourself, writing slides and diagrams as code, AI-generated B-roll, voice cloning, plus editing and captions.

My takeaway from the numbers is to film the talking head yourself, clone your voice for fixes, write slides as code and buy AI B-roll by the second. Under the assumptions in the report, a finished 10-minute video comes out to about \$0 to \$10 on the cheapest pipeline, \$60 to \$75 on the best-value one, and \$122 to \$230 on the top-quality one. In every paid pipeline, B-roll seconds make up the biggest part of the bill.

I also worked out a two-tier workflow. You iterate on the script and timing with a cheap draft voice like Kokoro, and preview shots in draft video mode, then render the final voice and only the chosen shots once. Under the report's assumptions, drafting B-roll pays for itself within two review rounds for every tool pair I modeled. Tiering the voice saves more time than money.

I checked every price directly on the vendor's page on 2026-10-25, and each claim sits in a table in the report with its source. I didn't generate any video, avatar or voice myself, so quality isn't measured here. Wherever I give an opinion on quality in the report, it's labelled as judgment.

The report also notes what changed this year (Sora is gone, and the Veo 3.1 preview on the Gemini API has closed), YouTube's rules on disclosing synthetic content, and the prices I couldn't verify.
