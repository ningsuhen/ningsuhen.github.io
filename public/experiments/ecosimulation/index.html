What this is

EcoSimulation is a Monte-Carlo engine that simulates a network of companies, countries and financial markets quarter by quarter. Cash and business flows are conserved: every payment has a payer and a payee, and accounting identities are checked every tick. The engine draws 50,000 paths from a fixed seed and exports 10,000 of them. This page lets you condition on those exported paths and watch six series re-fan.

The question

Given a state of the world, where do growth, jobs, markets and credit go?

Status

Live. Conditioning runs in the browser; re-simulation runs the engine in a Web Worker.

How to read it

Wider bands mean more uncertainty. A thin sample (few effective paths) is flagged, not hidden.

The headline

Across the exported 10,000 paths, the model puts the odds of a US recession at 9% within 6 months, 23% within 12 months, 43% within 24 months and 80% within 60 months. The median path is a slow fade, not a crash: growth drifts negative by 2028 and the S&P 500 drawdown of 30% or more is a 42% event over five years.

A dashed grey line is the unconditional median, so you can see how a filter moves the path. Open the Explore tab to set the filters.

The mechanism

The engine is a directed graph of entities: countries, regions, industries and 1,500 economically significant companies. Each tick it produces, settles payments, updates balance sheets and re-prices markets. Two numbers drive the whole thing: how much AI capability is available, and how much it is actually used. Everything else — capex, revenue, credit, employment — follows from those and from the macro state.

Conservation
Every payment has a payer and a payee; the sum of cash across the network is conserved up to the model's own shocks.
Significance-first
Only the top-N entities by economic weight are simulated (N configurable; target 1,500 companies), not everything.
Point-in-time
The model reads data only through a PIT reader with an explicit as_of, so it never sees the future.
Vector-first
NumPy/JAX operations over loops; the filter kernel is a single weighted sort.

Data and provenance

Initial conditions are observed where they exist (GDP, unemployment, core PCE, policy rate, the 10-year, HY OAS, oil, the S&P 500) and derived where they do not (the AI index, the output gap, the term basis). World growth is the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 vintage. The tree and parameters are guide-owned model config, not ingested data. The exported bundle is hashed and the engine's WASM is pinned to a SHA-256, so the numbers you see are reproducible.

Ruled-out combinations

Some combinations are logically impossible, so the model rules them out and the explorer greys the chip. For example, a full block on frontier open weights removes legal mainstream distribution, so it forbids a high local-AI share and an open model overtaking the regulated ones. A sector restriction forbids open weights overtaking in regulated industries. These rules are part of the bundle, not an assumption of this page.

How to use it

Two ways to ask the model a question. Condition filters the 10,000 exported paths — "given this driver state, what happens?" — and runs entirely in the browser. Intervene re-runs the engine with that state forced, drawing 5,000 fresh paths in a Web Worker — "what happens if we set it?" Re-simulated paths never replace the headline; they are labelled as an intervention.

Tap a state once to keep paths in it (in), twice to drop them (out), a third time to clear. Greyed states are ruled out by the current selection.

What the bands mean

A fan is a distribution over time, not a single forecast. The outer band (p5–p95) is where 90% of the selected paths land; the inner band (p25–p75) is the middle half; the median is the 50th percentile. A wide band is not an error — it is the model saying the outcome is genuinely uncertain. When a filter leaves few effective paths, the page says so instead of drawing a band that looks more precise than it is.

The quantile rule

Quantiles are weighted, not interpolated. The kernel sorts the selected values, walks the cumulative share of path weights, and returns the first value whose cumulative share reaches the target. That is the same rule the engine's Rust kernel uses, and the browser port is verified against it on 400 random filter specs. "Effective n" is the usual (Σw)²/Σw², the number of independent paths the sample is worth.

What is unvalidated

Read this first

This is a model, not a forecast. The numbers are conditional on a set of assumptions about AI capability, adoption and policy that have not been tested against the future. Treat the bands as a map of the model's own uncertainty, not as a probability the world will do this.
  • The AI-revenue and capex series are the least validated: they rest on company guidance and a small number of disclosed figures.
  • Intervention (re-simulation) numbers are a separate, smaller sample (5,000 paths) and are never mixed into the headline.

Limits

The model is significance-first: it simulates the top ~1,500 companies and aggregates the rest. Small firms, most of the world's economy, and tail events outside the parameterisation are not individually represented. The horizon is five years; anything beyond that is extrapolation the model does not attempt. Conditioning is a filter over a fixed export, so it cannot discover paths the export does not contain — only re-simulation can.

Code and engine

The engine is a Rust crate compiled to WebAssembly; the filter kernel (select, weighted quantile, effective n) is ported to JavaScript and verified against the WASM on 400 random filter specs. The exported bundle and the engine are pinned by SHA-256, so a re-run reproduces these numbers. The full report with the live engine, 500 source facts and the scenario presets is published alongside this experiment.
